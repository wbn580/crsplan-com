## Key Information Fields for CRS Reporting

For Hong Kong bank accounts subject to CRS, reporting entities must collect and report specific data. The key fields include:

- **Account holder information**: Name, address, tax identification number (TIN), and date of birth for individuals.
- **Account details**: Account number, account balance or value at year-end, and any income (interest, dividends, etc.) paid into the account.
- **Tax residency**: Each account holder must declare their tax residency status. This includes indicating the countries or territories where they are tax residents.
- **Control person information**: For accounts held by entities, the bank must identify individuals who exercise control over the entity (control persons). This includes owners with significant control and senior managing officials.

## Common Errors in CRS Reporting

Frequent mistakes can lead to penalties or compliance issues. Commonly seen errors include:

![Documents related to CRS reporting for Hong Kong bank accounts](https://img.ulec.com.cn/跨境法务-CRS合规/crs-2026-08-28-04c8324c90-image-02-2026-1880x1253.jpg)


- **Incorrect tax residency status**: Failing to update tax residency after a change, or declaring residency in only one jurisdiction when the holder is resident in multiple.
- **Misidentification of control persons**: For entity accounts, missing or incorrectly identifying individuals who are control persons.
- **Missing or invalid TINs**: Omitting TINs or providing incorrect ones for account holders.
- **Balance/value errors**: Reporting incorrect account balances or values, particularly when multiple currencies are involved.

These errors often stem from incomplete documentation or failure to periodically refresh customer information.

## Review Checklist for Practitioners

When preparing or reviewing CRS reports for Hong Kong bank accounts, consider the following points:

- **Verify the account holder's tax residency declaration** against supporting documentation, such as tax residency certificates or passports indicating residency.
- **Confirm control person identification** for entity accounts. Ensure all required fields are accurately completed.
- **Check that TINs are provided** for all individuals, and that they are valid and consistent with the jurisdiction of tax residency.
- **Cross-check account balances and values** against year-end statements, converting where necessary to the reporting currency.
- **Ensure completeness**—make sure every reportable account is included and no fields are left blank.

By systematically reviewing these fields and common pitfalls, cross-border legal practitioners can help clients meet their CRS obligations more accurately.
