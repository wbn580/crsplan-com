## The Core Documents for CRS Filing in Hong Kong

When a Hong Kong financial institution prepares a Common Reporting Standard (CRS) report for an account holder, it must collect a specific set of documents to determine the account holder's tax residency and to populate the report accurately. The following items are typically required:

- **Tax Residency Proof**: A document that establishes the account holder's country or countries of tax residence, such as a tax residency certificate or a self-certification form.
- **Address Proof**: A document that verifies the account holder's residential address, which is used as part of the due diligence process.
- **Tax Identification Number (TIN)**: The account holder's TIN for each jurisdiction of tax residence. If the TIN is not available, the institution may request an explanation.
- **Self-Certification Form**: A statement provided by the account holder that declares their tax residency status and other relevant information.

## Purpose and Importance of Each Document

Each document serves a specific purpose in the CRS process:

![A desk with a checklist of documents for CRS reporting.](https://img.ulec.com.cn/跨境法务-CRS合规/crs-tin-crs-2026-09-09-096ea215eb-image-02-2026-1880x1253.jpg)


- **Tax residency proof** allows the institution to determine which jurisdictions the account holder should be reported to, as CRS requires reporting to the tax authorities of the account holder's tax residence.
- **Address proof** helps in the due diligence process to confirm the account holder's location and to support the identification of tax residency, though it is not the sole basis for that determination.
- **TIN** is a unique identifier used by tax authorities to track a person's tax obligations. Providing the correct TIN ensures that the reported information is linked to the right taxpayer in the correct jurisdiction.
- **The self-certification form** is a declaration that places responsibility on the account holder to provide accurate information and helps the institution meet its due diligence obligations.

## How to Obtain These Documents

- **Tax residency certificate**: Issued by the tax authority of the country where the person is tax resident. The process varies by jurisdiction.
- **Address proof**: Can be a utility bill, bank statement, or government-issued document that shows the account holder's name and current address.
- **TIN**: Assigned by the tax authority upon registration for tax purposes. A person may have multiple TINs if they are tax resident in more than one country.
- **Self-certification form**: Provided by the financial institution; the account holder must complete it with accurate information.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Incorrect TIN**: Providing an outdated or incorrect TIN can lead to mismatches in the system and may result in penalties.
- **Incomplete self-certification**: Failing to declare all jurisdictions of tax residency, or not signing the form, can cause delays or a default to a different status.
- **Address proof not current**: An outdated address may not be sufficient; the institution may require recent documentation.
- **Mismatched information**: If the documents do not align with the information provided on the self-certification, the institution may need to ask for clarification.

## Comparison with Other Financial Centers: Singapore and Switzerland

While the underlying CRS framework is global, the specific document requirements can vary by jurisdiction. Differences may exist in the exact forms or types of documentation accepted.

- **Hong Kong**: Requires tax residency proof, address proof, TIN, and a self-certification. The process follows the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the CRS rules.
- **Singapore**: Similar to Hong Kong, but the specifics of the self-certification and acceptable address proof might differ. Singapore requires a tax residency certificate or equivalent.
- **Switzerland**: Known for its strict documentation requirements. Account holders may need to provide additional evidence of tax residency, and the self-certification process may be more detailed.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is a TIN and why is it required?
A Tax Identification Number (TIN) is a unique identifier assigned by a tax authority to individuals for tax purposes. It is required under CRS to ensure that the reported account information is attributed to the correct taxpayer.

### Can I use a bank statement as address proof?
Yes, in many cases a bank statement is accepted as proof of address, but it should show your current address and be recent.

### How often do I need to update my self-certification?
You should update your self-certification whenever there is a change in your tax residency status or other relevant information.

### Do I need to provide tax residency proof for every country?
If you are tax resident in more than one country, you must provide the relevant documentation and TINs for each jurisdiction.

### What happens if I provide incomplete or incorrect information?
The financial institution may need to contact you for clarification, and in some cases, you may face penalties for non-compliance.
