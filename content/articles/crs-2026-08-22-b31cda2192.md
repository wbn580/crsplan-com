## Overview
This article reviews common data errors observed in past CRS filings, based on case studies. The errors include incomplete account holder information, incorrect balance calculations, and misjudgment of tax residency status. Each error type is analyzed for its causes, and practical checklists and correction processes are provided to help tax preparers and legal assistants identify risks before submission.

## Incomplete Account Holder Information
A frequent error is incomplete account holder details, such as missing or incorrect name, address, or identification numbers. This can lead to mismatches with tax authority records and may trigger compliance issues. Ensure all mandatory fields are populated and verified against official documents.

## Balance Calculation Errors
Another common issue is incorrect account balances, which can arise from currency conversion mistakes, failure to include accrued interest, or using the wrong valuation date. For example, a case involved an account balance that was reported using an outdated exchange rate, resulting in an understatement. Always use the end-of-year exchange rate as per the guidance and include all income accrued up to the reporting date.

## Tax Residency Misjudgment
Misjudging an account holder's tax residency is a critical error, often stemming from misunderstood tie-breaker rules or unawareness of multiple residency statuses. This can lead to incorrect reporting jurisdiction. It is essential to apply the correct tie-breaker tests and consider all relevant facts, such as permanent home, center of vital interests, and habitual abode.

## Correction Process
When errors are identified, a structured correction process should be followed. This includes documenting the error, notifying the relevant authorities if necessary, submitting a corrected report, and retaining evidence of the correction. Regular self-review checklists can help minimize such errors before submission.
