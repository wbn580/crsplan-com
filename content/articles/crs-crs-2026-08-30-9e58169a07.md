## Understanding Your CRS Reporting Obligations

Under the Common Reporting Standard (CRS), financial institutions in participating jurisdictions are required to report information on financial accounts held by tax residents of other participating jurisdictions. If you are a high-net-worth individual with cross-border financial accounts, you may be subject to these reporting obligations. Your obligations depend on your tax residency status, the type of accounts you hold, and the jurisdictions involved.

## Determining Your Tax Residency

The first step is to accurately determine your tax residency. Tax residency is generally based on your personal circumstances, such as where you have a permanent home, your center of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality. Many jurisdictions also apply a day-count test. For example, if you are present in a country for a certain number of days, you may be considered a tax resident. It is essential to understand the rules of each jurisdiction where you have ties, as dual residency may arise, and tie-breaker rules apply under tax treaties. For a comprehensive checklist on tax residency, refer to our [Tax Residency for Cross-Border Residents: A CRS-Focused Checklist](/posts/crs-2026-08-07-f940758c03/).

## Collecting Your Financial Account Information

Next, you need to compile the required information for all your financial accounts. This includes your name, address, tax identification number (TIN), date of birth, and country of tax residency. For accounts held jointly, information on each account holder is required. Financial institutions will report details such as the account number, account balance or value, and income earned (interest, dividends, etc.). You should obtain your account statements and ensure they reflect the required data. It is important to verify that the information on file with each financial institution is accurate and consistent with your tax residency status. See our [Cross-Border Preparer's CRS Data Validation Checklist](/posts/crs-2026-08-01-f4d6894a8d/) for a practical guide.

## Completing and Submitting Your CRS Forms

Once your information is collected, you must complete the appropriate CRS reporting forms. These forms vary by jurisdiction but typically require you to declare your tax residency and provide the account details. Some countries require you to report your assets directly, while others mandate that financial institutions report on your behalf. You must ensure that all fields are correctly filled, as errors can lead to penalties. Double-check your TIN, account numbers, and balances. If you are unsure about your obligations, consult a tax professional familiar with CRS rules.

## Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

Common mistakes include providing an incorrect tax residency status, omitting accounts, or reporting inaccurate balances. For example, individuals may fail to include accounts held through trusts or entities. Another common error is not updating a change of address or tax residency in a timely manner. To avoid these pitfalls, review your data against account statements and retain supporting documentation. For a case-based review of common errors, see our [Common CRS Data Errors and How to Correct Them: A Case-Based Review](/posts/crs-2026-08-06-c519a05141/).

## Deadlines and Next Steps

CRS reporting deadlines vary by jurisdiction. Typically, financial institutions must report by a specific date each year, but as an individual, you may need to file a declaration with your tax authority by a certain deadline, often the same as your annual tax return. It is crucial to keep track of the deadlines in all relevant countries. Set reminders and start the process early to avoid last-minute issues. If you have already missed a deadline or need to correct a prior filing, there may be processes to rectify errors; see our guide on [CRS Reporting: Correcting Omitted Offshore Financial Accounts](/posts/crs-2026-08-18-a829ae0bc7/) for more information.

By systematically following these steps, you can meet your CRS compliance obligations and reduce the risk of penalties. Always seek professional advice tailored to your specific circumstances.
